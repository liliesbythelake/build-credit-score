Choose your language

What it is. A credit score is a number, usually between 300 and 850, that estimates how likely you are to repay borrowed money on time. Lenders, landlords, and sometimes employers use it to decide whether to approve you and what interest rate or deposit to offer.

Why it matters here. A landlord may check it before renting to you. A car loan or mortgage rate can shift by thousands of dollars depending on your score. Some utility and cell phone providers waive deposits for people with good scores. The US is a loan-driven system, so this number follows you into many everyday decisions.

The three bureaus. Three companies, Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, each keep their own record of your credit activity and calculate their own version of your score. They do not always agree, since lenders don't all report to all three. This is why your score can look slightly different depending on where you check it.

Where to check it for free. AnnualCreditReport.com is the only site required by federal law to give you free reports from all three bureaus. Many banks and credit cards also show a free score estimate in their app once you have an account.

What moves it most. Paying on time matters most, followed by how much of your available credit you're using, then the length of your credit history, the mix of account types you have, and how often you apply for new credit.
